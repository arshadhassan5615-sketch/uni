# BRIEF: "Reading the Forest: Deforestation Seen Through Digital Maps"

- **Slug:** reading-the-forest
- **Intent:** Educational explainer for a university presentation (professor + students).
- **Audience:** Academic audience, non-specialist in remote sensing. Expects sources and honesty about uncertainty.
- **Format:** 1920x1080, 30 fps, MP4. Target runtime 6:30 to 7:00 (hard minimum 6:00).
- **Narration:** Fish Audio, voice 2947ec32c7e1479c8ec5628a1fc035f1, model s2.1-pro-free. English.
- **Captions:** Minimal lower-third key numbers on screen; no karaoke captions. Full source slide at the end.
- **Core rule:** Every number on screen or in narration is in FACT-SHEET.md as VERIFIED, with source and year on screen.
- **Visual spine (video-storytelling skill):** ONE persistent world: a single Amazon map. The camera moves between
  world (whole Amazon), region (the arc of deforestation), detail (one number). Nothing is drawn that is not labelled.
- **Map data:** Real. Hansen/UMD Global Forest Change v1.12 (loss year 2001-2024) rendered by me from the official tiles,
  plus INPE PRODES polygons. No invented geometry.
- **Not included (no verified source):** causes by commodity, solutions/policy claims, regional numbers for Congo and
  Southeast Asia, the 2004 and 2012 Amazon rates. Said on the closing slide as "what this video does not claim".
- **Pacing:** Relaxed (3 to 5 s per idea), stoic voice, long holds on maps.
- **Music:** None by default (silence suits a stoic read and avoids licensing risk). Optional low ambient bed if you supply one.
- **Assets needed from you (optional):** real satellite photographs (NASA Earth Observatory / Landsat) for the "how a satellite sees" scene, with credit lines.
